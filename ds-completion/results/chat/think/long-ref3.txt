Suppose, for contradiction, that \(\sqrt{2}\) is rational. Then we can write
\[
\sqrt{2}=\frac{a}{b},
\]
where \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\), \(b\neq 0\), and \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) (the fraction is in lowest terms).

Squaring both sides gives
\[
2=\frac{a^2}{b^2},
\]
so
\[
a^2=2b^2.
\]
Thus \(a^2\) is even. Since the square of an integer is even exactly when the integer is even, \(a\) must be even. Write
\[
a=2k
\]
for some integer \(k\). Substituting,
\[
(2k)^2=2b^2,
\]
so
\[
4k^2=2b^2,
\]
and hence
\[
b^2=2k^2.
\]
Therefore \(b^2\) is even, so \(b\) is even.

Thus both \(a\) and \(b\) are divisible by \(2\), contradicting \(\gcd(a,b)=1\). Hence no such representation exists, and therefore
\[
\boxed{\sqrt{2}\text{ is irrational}.}
\]

### \(\sqrt{3}\) is irrational

Now suppose, for contradiction, that \(\sqrt{3}\) is rational. Then
\[
\sqrt{3}=\frac{c}{d},
\]
where \(c,d\in\mathbb Z\), \(d\neq 0\), and \(\gcd(c,d)=1\).

Squaring gives
\[
3=\frac{c^2}{d^2},
\]
so
\[
c^2=3d^2.
\]
Thus \(3\mid c^2\). Since \(3\) is prime, if \(3\mid c^2\), then \(3\mid c\). Write
\[
c=3m
\]
for some integer \(m\). Substituting,
\[
(3m)^2=3d^2,
\]
so
\[
9m^2=3d^2,
\]
and hence
\[
d^2=3m^2.
\]
Therefore \(3\mid d^2\), and since \(3\) is prime, \(3\mid d\).

Thus both \(c\) and \(d\) are divisible by \(3\), contradicting \(\gcd(c,d)=1\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{\sqrt{3}\text{ is irrational}.}
\]
