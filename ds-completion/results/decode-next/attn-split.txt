# commands: bin-hip/bench-xdie 6 / 2 (hand-offs), bin-hip/bench-xdie proj (wo_a + wo_b), the A/B runs and
# the trace below with the flags of scripts/spec-measure.sh (--spec --canon --cache-loads 800 --cache-period 8 --cache-wait) +/- --attn-split

What the patch does (--attn-split, M <= 8 only): die 1 holds wo_a's groups 4-7 and wo_b's rows
2560-5119 (1.41 GiB for the 40 layers); per layer die 0 sends o's codes for heads 32-63, both dies run
their half of wo_a, swap their halves of oa's codes, run their half of wo_b, and die 1's rows come
back. Hand-offs: a put kernel copies into a fine-grained mailbox on the other die and raises a flag
there; a get kernel spins on the local flag and copies into ordinary memory. No events, no host.
The GEMV takes row strides (ldx, ldy) and the whole matrix's lanes per row, so every row has the
one-die bits.

polled by a waiting kernel costs the GPU ~6-8 us and the host ~3.

Bits: the split's output equals the one-die output at every M. The split's fixed cost is
~55 us per layer (M = 6: 268 -> 190 where halving would give ~134): die 0 runs 4 more kernels
(two puts, two gets) and waits twice for die 1, which cannot start before o's codes arrive.
The host is not the limit (24 us of submits per layer against 126-278 us of GPU time).

## Conclusion

Exact, but +0.6%: not kept. What would change it: drop die 0's copy of the halves (the prompt's
fp8_gemm would need the strides and the split too) to get the cache back (est. +1.5-2%), or split
the attention core and q_b by heads as well, so die 1 makes its own heads' o and the largest
transfer goes away (needs the KV caches, the indexer's picks and speculation's rollback on die 1;
est. +4-6%, a larger change).
