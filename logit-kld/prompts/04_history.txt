The transition from sail to steam in the nineteenth century did not happen overnight, and for several decades the two technologies coexisted in an uneasy balance. Early steamships were coal-hungry and their engines unreliable, so they were confined to rivers, coastal routes, and subsidized mail runs, while sailing clippers still dominated the long ocean trades where coaling stations were scarce.
