The history of cities is, in large part, the history of water. Rome grew because its aqueducts could carry more than the Tiber offered; Amsterdam turned its canals into streets and its streets into markets; Chicago reversed an entire river to keep its drinking supply clean. When a city outgrows its water, it either builds or declines — and the record of which choice a civilization made is usually still visible centuries later in its stones and maps.
